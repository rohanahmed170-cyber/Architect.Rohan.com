ARC 402中庭

Aangina

Community-based housing with integrated livelihoods

Studio
ARC 402
Site
Korail, Mohakhali, Dhaka
Setting
Urban water edge
Programme
Housing, community spaces, livelihood facilities

Aangina means courtyard in Bengali. This community-based housing complex sits on an urban water edge in Korail, Mohakhali, and brings housing, community space and livelihood facilities into one environment. The idea comes from the South Asian courtyard, a shared open space that becomes the social heart of a community, reinterpreted here as a network of linked courtyards between housing blocks.

Ground floors hold small workshops, shops and community halls to draw activity to street level. Upper floors step back for light, ventilation and views toward the water. The massing moved from a simple grid of solid blocks to the final courtyard arrangement, shaped by context, wind direction and views.

ARC 501

Wellness Centre at Gol Talab

A brick wellness centre in the historic fabric of Old Dhaka

Studio
ARC 501
Site
Gol Talab, Old Dhaka
Context
Near Ahsan Manzil, the Buriganga and Islampur
Acknowledged
Abul Fazal Mahmudun Nobi, Mahmudul Islam Chowdhury

A wellness centre on the edge of Gol Talab in Old Dhaka, close to Ahsan Manzil, the Buriganga and the Islampur riverport. The site study began with how people already reach the water. Inner paths and gaps between buildings lead to the open void of Gol Talab, used mostly by residents and workers for bathing and washing, apart from the commercial traffic of Islampur Road.

Two brick building groups are organised around planted courtyards and linked at upper level by an arcaded, planted bridge, over a basement car park. Materials follow a survey of the surroundings: exposed brick, painted and lime plaster, timber doors and windows. At concept stage, an AI-assisted workshop (inpainting and LoRA conditioning on a site photograph) was used to explore massing in context.

ARC 401

Water Urbanism at Kaundia

Community-based water urbanism on the peri-urban edge of Dhaka

Studio
ARC 401
Site
Kaundia, Savar, Dhaka
Scale
Settlement, neighbourhood and building
Group work
Vision framework by Group 5(B)
Acknowledged
Khondaker Hasibul Kabir, Khurshid Zabin Hossain Taufique, Imon Chowdhooree, Tasfin Aziz, Ruaida Akter

Kaundia is a small union in Savar, west of Dhaka near the Turag River, where farmland and spreading settlement meet rivers, canals and floodplains. The studio asked how this edge can grow while keeping its water systems intact. Mapping from 2010 to 2025 shows the land and water pattern changing, with flooding, poor drainage and unplanned growth as the main pressures.

Group 5(B) set a vision for a resilient urban edge with better connectivity, richer biodiversity and improved health facilities: new roads, bridges and ghat-to-ghat links, floating farming, urban parks and distributed healthcare. The settlement master plan adds wet markets, ghats, waste management, sports and wellness centres, a boatmaking and exhibition zone and social spaces. At neighbourhood scale, a sports club for a low-income community offers coaching, equipment access and workshops beside the playing field, with green terraces, street markets and natural ventilation.

ARC 302

Sylvan Skyscraper

A high-rise grown from the form of a tree

Studio
ARC 302
Site
Site 1, DNCC Market, Gulshan 1, Dhaka
Programme
High-rise
Faculty
Abul Fazal Mahmudun Nobi, Atiqur Rahman, Nabi Nawaz Khan, Tanveer Ahmed

Sylvan Skyscraper starts from a tree: a cluster of circles lifted into cylinders of different heights, joined by a podium. The tallest volume shifts its floor plates as it rises, opening planted terraces, while columns lift the lower volumes and leave the ground level open. The site is the DNCC Market in Gulshan 1.

Site analysis of the existing market recorded visitor numbers by day and hour, how space is used, noise (77 to 109 dB near the road, 65 to 74 dB inside) and traffic. Men's clothing was the largest use, with garments and godowns next, and the poorly lit, poorly ventilated areas were read as wasted space. A case study of the HSBC Tower in Hong Kong, by Norman Foster, looked at its mast, truss and suspended-floor structure.

ARC 301

Interpretation and Edutainment

Two short projects: a nature interpretation centre and a children's edutainment centre

Studio
ARC 301, Spring 2023
Sites
Lakshmipur; Mohakhali TB Gate, Dhaka
Duration
Four weeks and six weeks

The Nature Interpretation Centre (four weeks, Lakshmipur) places small circular pavilions in a palm grove at the water's edge, reached by one winding path. Interpretation centres share knowledge of natural and cultural heritage through exhibitions and interactive, multimedia programmes, and the path was tested in physical models.

The Edutainment Centre (six weeks, Mohakhali TB Gate) is a place for children to learn through exploration, discovery and imagination. A multipurpose hall and auditorium, exhibition hall, learning spaces and a rooftop café are arranged around planted courtyards, with a perforated wall on the east facade and ramps and stairs linking the levels beside the water.

Café and Lounge Interior

Interior architecture study

Type
Interior design
Programme
Café, bar and lounge
Materials
Brick, timber, planting

An interior study for a café and lounge. Exposed brick columns and a timber deck floor set the tone under a suspended ceiling grid. Screens of timber poles, hanging climbers and planters divide the floor into seating zones, with a colour-banded bar, a reading corner with shelves beside the stair, and a graphic feature wall.

About

プロフィール

Hi, I'm Robiul Kawser, an architecture student at BRAC University in Dhaka. I look at how people, spaces and the built environment shape one another, with a steady interest in contextual architecture, community engagement and sustainable design.

I treat architecture as a process of understanding people, place and context. A good building does more than look right. It answers environmental pressures, carries cultural identity and serves the everyday needs of the people who use it. I aim to make architecture that is functional, responsive and meaningful.
